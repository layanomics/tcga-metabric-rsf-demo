Understanding C-index (Concordance Index)

C-index = 0.72 means the model correctly ranks patient risk 72% of the time

This is like asking: "If I give the model two random patients, can it correctly predict which one has higher risk?" Our model gets it right 72 out of 100 times.

Example: Correct Ranking

Patient A: Age 75, Stage 4

Model predicts: HIGH RISK ✓

Patient B: Age 35, Stage 1

Model predicts: LOW RISK ✓

✓ Correctly ranked! Patient A has higher risk.

What does 0.72 mean?

0.5 = Random guessing (coin flip)

0.7-0.8 = Good model (our model is here!)

0.8-0.9 = Excellent model

1.0 = Perfect ranking (impossible in practice)

Why is C-index important?

  • • It measures how well the model ranks patients by risk, not just predicts exact survival times
  • • More robust than other metrics for survival data with censoring (patients lost to follow-up)
  • • Clinical utility: helps identify which patients need more aggressive treatment
  • • Standard metric in survival analysis research

How Random Survival Forest Works

The Model

A Random Survival Forest is an ensemble of 200 decision trees that work together to predict survival outcomes. It's specifically designed for time-to-event data (survival analysis) rather than simple classification.

200 Trees

Each tree votes on the prediction

Max Depth: 5
